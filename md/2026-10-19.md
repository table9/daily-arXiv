### Noise-Induced Restoration of Entropy Concavity Near Gaussian Equilibrium
- **arXiv**: [2610.22386](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.22386)  ·  [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.22386.pdf)
- **Title (EN)**: Noise-Induced Restoration of Entropy Concavity Near Gaussian Equilibrium
- **Authors**: Fayez Abu-Ajamieh
- **Categories**: cond-mat.stat-mech, hep-th
- **TL;DR**: 本文研究一维非平衡态在高斯热平衡松弛过程中熵凹性的噪声诱导恢复，给出端点曲率的精确公式并证明对称Hermite模在全混合参数范围内熵严格凹。

We study a quantitative form of noise-induced restoration of entropy concavity for one-dimensional nonequilibrium states relaxing toward a Gaussian thermal equilibrium. A partially thermalized state is modeled by $X_q=\sqrt q\,X+\sqrt{1-q}\,G$, where $G$ is Gaussian equilibrium noise and $q$ is the retained signal fraction. For two independently prepared copies we ask when the entropy of the mixed state $h(\sqrt{1-t}\,X_q+\sqrt t\,Y_q)$ is concave in the mixing parameter. The problem is motivated by entropy geometry along Ornstein--Uhlenbeck/Fokker--Planck relaxation, where the relative score is the nonequilibrium thermodynamic force and relative Fisher information is entropy-production rate. We derive an exact symmetric endpoint-curvature formula, separating a nonnegative Ornstein--Uhlenbeck restoring term from a fourth-cumulant correction, and extend it to nonsmooth generalized-Gaussian states $e^{-a|x|^p}$ for $p&gt;3/2$. For half thermalization of a symmetric log-concave input, posterior-variance identities give a quantitative outer-region theorem confining any endpoint obstruction to two explicitly bounded near-equilibrium sectors. We also give spectral and MMSE reformulations of the auxiliary inequality $A\ge2δ$, prove it in an explicit nonlinear neighborhood of Gaussian equilibrium, and show how it sharpens the endpoint bounds. The main all-mixing result concerns the leading symmetric non-Gaussian Hermite mode: for $p_a=\phiG(1+a\He_4)$, $0&lt;a\le10^{-2}$, we prove strict entropy concavity for every $0&lt;t&lt;1$ and every $0&lt;q\le5/8$, hence throughout the currently unresolved symmetric range $0&lt;q\le4/7$ for this explicit nonequilibrium family. Numerical searches over broader log-concave families are reported only as supporting evidence.

### Thermodynamics of coupled electromagnetic and mass fields
- **arXiv**: [2610.22391](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.22391)  ·  [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.22391.pdf)
- **Title (EN)**: Thermodynamics of coupled electromagnetic and mass fields
- **Authors**: Fei Wang
- **Categories**: cond-mat.stat-mech
- **TL;DR**: 该文提出一个统一的热力学框架，通过将 Gibbs–Duhem 关系扩展以纳入动能自由能贡献，从而统一描述化学、力学与电磁输运。

We formulate a unified thermodynamic framework for chemical, mechanical, and electromagnetic transport by extending the Gibbs--Duhem relation to include kinetic free-energy contributions associated with material and field motion. The resulting kinetic Gibbs--Duhem relation establishes a thermodynamic connection between free-energy variations, generalized chemical potentials, pressure, and transport momentum, while preserving the invariance of the system free energy under changes of reference frame. Within a Lagrangian formulation of free-energy evolution, the relation provides a common structure for diffusion, momentum transport, and electromagnetic dynamics. For nonuniform chemical systems, the framework recovers the coupling between Cahn--Hilliard dynamics and the Korteweg stress arising from composition gradients. Its extension to electromagnetic systems incorporates electric, magnetic, charge, and mechanical contributions into the same thermodynamic formulation, yielding a unified description of electromagnetic and momentum evolution. The framework further separates dissipative from nondissipative transport and establishes a common thermodynamic form for mechanical and electromagnetic propagation. In the isentropic limit, the characteristic propagation velocity is determined by the derivative of the thermodynamic pressure with respect to density, yielding the speed of sound for mechanical systems and the speed of light for electromagnetic systems. These results show that kinetic contributions can be incorporated consistently into the Gibbs--Duhem framework, providing a common thermodynamic basis for coupled chemical, mechanical, and electromagnetic transport without introducing separate thermodynamic principles for each mechanism.
