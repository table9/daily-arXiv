### Correlated quantum criticality and topological magnetoelectric response in Bi2Se3
- **arXiv**: [2611.03670](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.03670)  ·  [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.03670.pdf)
- **Title (EN)**: Correlated quantum criticality and topological magnetoelectric response in Bi2Se3
- **Authors**: Aniekan Magnus Ukpong
- **Categories**: cond-mat.str-el
- **TL;DR**: 将Bi₂Se₃的拓扑表面态映射为含阻挫J₁–J₂交换的蜂窝Hubbard模型，用单一控制参数耦合磁序与拓扑态，发现Néel与价键固体序参量在J₂/J₁≈0.22交叉且能带反转、Chern/ℤ₂跳变与skyrmion荷Q同步，从而给出Q—轴子角—拓扑磁电耦合的微观联系并估算每skyrmion每周期~10⁻²⁰J的能量。

A defining feature of quantum materials is that multiple orders govern low-energy physics and are not independent. We study Bi$_2$Se$_3$ by combining DFT with a renormalized correlated honeycomb model in which frustrated magnetic order and helical topological surface states are coupled via one control parameter. The surface states map to a honeycomb Hubbard model with frustrated $J_1$--$J_2$ exchange. Exact diagonalization up to 20 sites shows the Néel structure factor and valence-bond-solid order parameter cross at $J_2/J_1 \approx 0.22$, agreeing with QMC/DMRG, and a dynamical spin structure factor broadens near the crossover, as expected for a spinon continuum. Band inversion of the renormalized tight-binding model occurs on the same critical locus, producing jumps in the Chern number and $\mathbb{Z}_2$ invariant mirrored by the skyrmion charge $Q$. This gives a microscopic link: $Q$ selects the axion angle and thus the topological magnetoelectric coupling. We distinguish axion coupling, independent of skyrmion helicity, from the helicity-dependent magnetoelectric multipole coupling, whose toroidal component scales as $\sinγ$ and vanishes for Néel-type textures. We derive the $Q$-topological magnetoelectric energy and estimate $\sim 10^{-20}\,\mathrm{J}$ per skyrmion per cycle for few-tesla fields. The cycle is thermodynamically bounded: constant-$θ$ cycles extract no net work, and efficiency is limited by axionic entropy change.

### When do machine-learning potentials need a configuration-dependent kernel? Learning long-range interactions with Fourier neural operators
- **arXiv**: [2611.03678](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.03678)  ·  [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.03678.pdf)
- **Title (EN)**: When do machine-learning potentials need a configuration-dependent kernel? Learning long-range interactions with Fourier neural operators
- **Authors**: Wei Chen, Gian-Marco Rignanese
- **Categories**: cond-mat.mtrl-sci, physics.chem-ph
- **TL;DR**: 该文用傅里叶神经算子(FNO)从MACE局部描述符构建的潜在源场中学习长程相互作用核，证明当屏蔽介质非均匀时，依赖构型的核是必要的。

Long-range interactions in machine-learning interatomic potentials are commonly treated by coupling learned sources through either a prescribed electrostatic kernel or a learned but configuration-independent one. Whether such a kernel suffices depends on the underlying screened interaction. We address this question by using Fourier neural operators (FNOs) to learn how latent source fields, built from the descriptors of a local backbone (here MACE), interact across the simulation cell. For a synthetic ionic system with tunable screening, a prescribed Coulomb kernel underestimates the long-wavelength charge fluctuations by 43% under strong screening, whereas the FNO reproduces them within 3% and recovers 95-99% of the variance of the residual left by the local backbone. A linear FNO, which learns a configuration-independent kernel, performs equally well in the homogeneous case. Under inhomogeneous screening, rearranging the remote medium modulates the coupling between distant ions, a dependence that no configuration-independent kernel can represent. Among the models tested, only the nonlinear FNO responds to this modulation, and its force errors are 27-41% lower than those of configuration-independent models. It further captures the reversal of the Au2 wetting preference induced by remote Al dopants, with deviations below 5 meV per cell from density functional theory. These results indicate that a configuration-dependent kernel, as learned by FNOs, becomes necessary once the screening medium varies, even though the energy remains quadratic in the charges for a fixed medium.
